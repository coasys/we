# Hero Copy Variations

---

## Option A — Interface layer, plain and precise

**Title:** An open environment for community-built interfaces.

**Tagline:** Most platforms give you a space to use. WE gives you an environment to shape.

**Body:** Today's platforms own your data, dictate your interface, and lock out innovation. WE is different: mix modules from an open marketplace, design experiences your community actually needs, and own your infrastructure. What one community builds, every community can use. Fork it, remix it, share it back.

---

## Option B — Living web, forward-looking

**Title:** The interface layer for the living web.

**Tagline:** Design your community's experience. Own what you build. Share what works.

**Body:** Most platforms are privately owned, opaquely governed, and structurally resistant to change. WE is an open environment built on a different premise — where communities compose their own interfaces from shared modules, own their data infrastructure, and contribute back to a growing commons. Collective intelligence, without loss of coherence.

---

## Option C — WE as pronoun, collaborative angle

**Title:** The community software WE build together.

**Tagline:** Most platforms give you a space to use. WE gives you an environment to shape.

**Body:** Today's platforms control our data, dictate our interfaces, and capture the value communities create. WE is the alternative: an open environment where communities design their own experiences, mix modules from a shared marketplace, and own their infrastructure. Fork what exists. Share what works. Let discoveries spread.

---

## Option D — Punchy, functional

**Title:** Build the community interface your community actually needs.

**Tagline:** Open modules. Owned data. Shared discoveries.

**Body:** Most platforms are rigid by design — they control your data, lock your interface, and treat your community as a product. WE is an open environment where you compose experiences from shared modules, own your infrastructure, and contribute back. What one community figures out, every community can use.

---

## Option E — Leans into the "living" quality

**Title:** Where community interfaces come alive.

**Tagline:** Communities don't just live in WE. They shape it.

**Body:** Most platforms control what your community can be. WE is an open environment where communities design their own interfaces, mix and remix modules from an open marketplace, and own their data — with no platform in between. Fork what works. Share what you build. Collective intelligence, without sacrificing what makes each community distinctly itself.

---

## Option F — Ownership tagline

**Title:** The open environment your community actually owns.

**Tagline:** Own your data. Shape your environment. Share what you build.

**Body:** Most platforms are privately owned, opaquely governed, and built to extract — not evolve with the communities that use them. WE is an open environment where you compose your community's interface from shared modules, own your data infrastructure, and contribute back to a growing commons. What one community discovers, every community can use. Collective intelligence, without losing what makes each community distinctly itself.

---

## Notes

- **B** is strongest if you want to establish the "living web" framing early — positions WE as a layer on top of something bigger (AD4M), which is accurate.
- **A** is the most precise and readable.
- **C** plays with the WE pronoun but risks feeling like a marketing line.
- "Collective intelligence, without loss of coherence" earns its place in whichever version — it addresses the implicit worry that open/decentralised means chaotic.
- "Social infrastructure for a more cooperative world" works better as a Coasys-level statement, not a WE headline.



We’ve grown used to platforms that harvest our data, dictate our interfaces, and block innovation. But it doesn’t have to be this way. WE offers an alternative: an open environment where communities can reclaim their infrastructure, customise their interfaces, mix and match the tools they need, and share the fruits of those experiments with others.
