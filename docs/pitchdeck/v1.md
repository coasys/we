## Slide 4

AD4M

At the foundation of our stack is the AD4m spanning layer.

Right now app developers are forced into picking one backend/data storage solution: a particular DB or protocol, that all users of their tools are forced into adopting.

This design constraint is so ubiquitous, most people aren't even aware that an alternative might be possible.

Problems:

- Apps tightly coupled to backend architecture: difficult to change if a better DB solution or protocol emerges later
- All communities/users forced to accept this data architecture even if not optimal to their needs
- Data lock-in: hard/impossible to take your data with you when switching apps
- Interoperability absent: hard for apps to communicate with each other – incompatible data structures

AD4M solves this by introducing a new data spanning layer – a meta-language/graph that can describe any data structure and connects to 'languages' that can map any data base/protocol to the graph.

Now app developers don't need to think about which DB to use. They build their app on the AD4M layer and their communities can freely choose how they want the data to be stored. Interoperability is now baked in. Data can move between ad4m apps & communities freely despite variations in underlying data structures. Communities can even switch apps and seemlessly bring their data with them.

## Slide 5

WE

At the next level of the stack we have WE which applies a similar pattern to the front end / interface layer.

Right now we're used to rigid/fixed interfaces: a one-size fits all approach forced on all users/ communities on an app. And developers/innovators who have an idea for a new tool are forced to build a full/siloed app just to bring their idea into the world, and then must compete against others for adoption (network effects).

Problems:

- Social tools can't be adapted to users/communities specific needs – a monoculture is enforced on everyone – despite the radical diversity of needs – even within a single community
- Massive duplication of efforts and unnecessary zero sum competition between developers who have compatible innovations
- Interoperability across apps and communities constrained due to incompatible UI frameworks & conventions

WE solves this by introducing a new UI spanning layer – a meta-language for describing interfaces (JSON template schemas), a set of building blocks & design conventions to maximise interoperability, and an open module marketplace where contributions can be freely shared, adopted, and upregulated based on use.

Advantages:

- Framework agnostic (SolidJS default, designed to work with React, Vue, Svelte etc)
- Safe to share (everything described in JSON)
- Easy to inspect, fork, remix in real-time/at runtime
- Works seamlessly with AI – no-code design

This gives users and communities the freedom to adapt the tools they use every day to meet their specific needs, without fragmenting across different silos. And allows developers to focus on the specific tools they want to build without duplicating other work or competing for network effects. Building blocks/modules can be shared & mixed and matched freely across all WE interfaces.

## Slide 6

Synergy

At the third level of the stack we have Synergy which applies the spanning layer logic to the problem of currencies and signals.

All social systems that scale beyond Dunbars number (everyone knows everyone) inevitably run into a fundamental coordination challenge – who can be trusted, for what, how can we exchange/signal value/meaning/beliefs across radically different contexts, and how can we align the incetives of the parts towards the goals of the whole?

Today we're used to rigid signalling systems that compress our values into binary signals, and incentivise actions that contradict our goals. Up/down votes, emoji reactions, and currencies that are disconnected from our values.

Synergy provides the infrastructure for communities to design their own currency & signalling systems specifically for their context, and provides a mechanism to translate those values across membranes/contexts.

Radical diversity without losing interpretability.
