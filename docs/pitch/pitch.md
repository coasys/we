# WE

## Communities deserve software they can evolve.

Today's online communities are far more dynamic than the platforms they depend on. People build cultures, governance structures, shared economies, and knowledge systems — but they do it inside tools they don't control, can't modify, and can't escape without losing everything.

WE is a shared environment where communities can build, evolve, and own the coordination software they actually need — not as a platform, but as a living ecosystem of interoperable experiences.

---

## The Cards

---

### 1. You're always a tenant

**The problem**

Every online community lives inside someone else's platform. You can configure what the platform gives you — maybe choose a theme, set some permissions, add a bot — but the deeper layers are fixed. The feed algorithm is fixed. The governance tools are fixed. The economic model is fixed. The interface is fixed.

When the platform changes its rules, raises its prices, optimises for engagement over value, or simply shuts down — the community loses everything. History, relationships, and accumulated knowledge disappear or are held hostage.

**What WE offers**

In WE, communities are not tenants. Their data lives in decentralised infrastructure they fully own, on nodes they control. No central server that can be taken down or policy-changed away. Uninstall an experience, your data stays. Switch tools, your history travels with you. The community holds the keys.

---

### 2. The fragmentation trap

**The problem**

Even communities that avoid big platforms often fragment across many separate tools — a chat app here, a governance tool there, voting somewhere else, payments in another place, shared docs scattered across a drive. Every time someone wants to do something new, the answer is "add another tool." Gradually, context, participation, and memory get spread so thin that coordination itself becomes exhausting.

Apps are also islands: each one has its own interface conventions, its own data model, its own login, its own learning curve. Good patterns discovered in one community can't easily spread to another. Innovation resets with every new product.

**What WE offers**

WE is a meta-app — one environment where many different experiences can coexist, interoperate, and be remixed together. A community doesn't bolt on a new tool; it evolves its environment. Discussion, governance, shared knowledge, economic coordination, and custom interfaces can all live in one place over one identity. Good ideas spread through the marketplace rather than staying locked inside individual products.

---

### 3. Your tools can't grow with you

**The problem**

Communities evolve. What works for 30 people breaks at 300. What works for casual discussion isn't enough for serious governance. What works for information-sharing doesn't support economic coordination. But most platforms are designed to stay the same — the product a community picked in year one is largely the product they're still using in year five.

Changing platforms means migrating. Migrating means losing history, re-onboarding every member, rebuilding workflows, and severing social continuity. So most communities stay stuck with tools they've outgrown because the cost of change is too high.

**What WE offers**

In WE, communities can adopt new governance mechanisms, moderation tools, interfaces, signalling systems, or economic flows without migrating platforms. A community might start with a simple voting mechanism, experience its limitations, switch to quadratic voting, and evolve from there — all without losing a single post or relationship. The environment grows as the community grows.

---

### 4. The AI generation paradox

**The problem**

AI is about to make it trivially easy to generate custom data models, apps, and interfaces on demand. But this creates a deep problem: if every community uses AI to generate its own custom schemas for posts, discussions, calendar events, and resources, they produce thousands of incompatible formats for the same concepts. Community A's "track" and Community B's "song" are structurally different even though they represent the same thing. Interoperability collapses exactly when flexibility explodes.

**What WE offers**

WE uses a standardised set of content building blocks — TextBlock, AudioBlock, ImageBlock, CollectionBlock, and ~20 others — as a shared vocabulary. Communities compose these blocks differently (and AI can compose them endlessly), but the blocks themselves are universal. Community A and Community B can read each other's content, query each other's libraries, and remix each other's structures — without any upfront coordination. The alphabet is fixed; the sentences are free.

---

### 5. Apps that are data, not code

**The problem**

Traditional apps are opaque. They're compiled code: you can use them, but you can't inspect them, modify them, safely fork them, or share just a piece of one. Customisation means either accepting the settings menu a developer left you, or convincing the developer to change the product. AI can describe what you want but can't reach inside the interface to actually change it.

**What WE offers**

WE experiences are defined by JSON schemas — declarative descriptions of layout, components, data bindings, and behaviour. This means experiences are:

- **Safe to share** — JSON is inert data, not executable code
- **Remixable** — extract a sidebar, a governance panel, a player bar, and splice them into your own layout
- **AI-editable** — "make the header purple and add a search bar" is a real request AI can execute by modifying JSON
- **Forkable** — take someone else's experience, make it your own, share your version back

The interface becomes something a community can actually sculpt, not just accept.

---

### 6. Signals that fit what your community cares about

**The problem**

Every platform gives you the same signal vocabulary: likes, upvotes, stars. These don't fit what most communities actually care about. A research community wants to signal methodology quality and relevance separately. A governance community wants weighted votes based on stake or expertise. A creative community wants different feedback signals than a news community. But the signals are hardcoded — take it or leave it.

**What WE offers**

Communities in WE define their own signal types — with custom names, icons, numeric scales, display styles (toggle, up/down, star rating, slider), and aggregation methods (count, sum, mean, median). A research community can have "methodological quality" and "practical relevance" as distinct signals. A community can start with a simple like and evolve to weighted voting without losing historical data. Because signal types carry a declared scale and semantic, signals from different communities can even be normalised and compared — cross-community content carries its context with it.

---

### 7. A marketplace that evolves the ecosystem

**The problem**

In today's software world, good coordination patterns get trapped inside individual products. If one community discovers a better way to surface important discussions, run a proposal process, or allocate shared resources — that discovery rarely escapes the product it was built inside. Knowledge gets locked; experimentation doesn't compound.

**What WE offers**

The WE marketplace distributes not just themes and widgets, but templates, governance mechanisms, signalling systems, block types, payment flows, moderation tools, and knowledge interfaces — as reusable, forkable, evolvable components. A community that develops a better participatory budgeting flow can publish it. Another community adopts and improves it. The pattern spreads. This is how social infrastructure should work: distributed experimentation with compounding improvement, not a few centralised product teams deciding what all communities get.

---

### 8. AI as collaborator, not passenger

**The problem**

AI assistants can describe software changes, suggest features, and write documentation — but they're structurally outside the product. They can't reshape the interface, modify the governance logic, or adapt the environment to a community's evolving needs. The locked, opaque nature of conventional apps makes AI a spectator.

**What WE offers**

WE is designed to be an environment AI can actively participate in shaping. Experiences are schema-driven and interpretable. Data has shared meaning through block types. The design system is documented and machine-readable. This gives AI a concrete surface to work with:

- Generate a new experience from a natural language description
- Edit a layout section in response to community feedback
- Propose a governance change and render it for preview
- Help communities discover and adopt relevant modules from the marketplace

AI becomes a collaborator in the ongoing evolution of the community's environment — not just a chatbot sitting alongside it.

---

## What this adds up to

Most online communities today are socially alive but structurally constrained. They can grow, create, and self-organise — but they can't meaningfully evolve the software conditions they live inside.

WE is an attempt to change that. Not by building a better platform, but by giving communities a shared environment where the tools themselves become something that can be owned, remixed, and evolved over time.

The deeper bet: if the right infrastructure exists, communities will discover forms of coordination, governance, and collective intelligence that no platform team would have designed for them — and those discoveries will spread.

That is what WE is for.
