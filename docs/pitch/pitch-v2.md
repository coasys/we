# WE

## Communities deserve software they can evolve.

Today's online communities are far more dynamic than the platforms they depend on. People build cultures, governance structures, shared economies, and knowledge systems — but they do it inside tools they don't control, can't modify, and can't escape without losing everything.

WE is a shared environment where communities can build, evolve, and own the coordination software they actually need — not as a platform, but as a living ecosystem of interoperable experiences.

---

### 1. You're always a tenant

Every online community lives inside someone else's platform. You can configure what the platform gives you — maybe choose a theme, set some permissions, add a bot — but the deeper layers are fixed. The feed algorithm is fixed. The governance tools are fixed. The economic model is fixed. The interface is fixed.

When the platform changes its rules, raises its prices, optimises for engagement over value, or simply shuts down — the community loses everything. History, relationships, and accumulated knowledge disappear or are held hostage.

In WE, communities are not tenants. Data lives on AD4M, decentralised infrastructure the community fully owns. No central server that can be taken down or policy-changed away. Uninstall an experience — your data stays. Switch tools — your history travels with you. The community holds the keys.

---

### 2. The fragmentation trap

Communities that avoid big platforms often end up fragmented across many separate tools: a chat app here, a governance tool there, voting somewhere else, shared docs scattered across a drive. Every new need means "add another tool." Gradually, context, participation, and memory get spread so thin that coordination becomes exhausting.

Each app is also an island — its own data model, its own interface conventions, its own learning curve. Good patterns discovered in one community stay locked inside one product. Innovation resets with every new selection.

WE is a meta-app: one environment where many experiences coexist, interoperate, and remix together. Discussion, governance, shared knowledge, and economic coordination all live in one place, over one identity. A community doesn't bolt on another tool — it evolves its environment.

---

### 3. Experiences you can actually change

Traditional apps are opaque. They're compiled, locked, and fixed — you can use them but you can't inspect them, remix them, or modify them. Customisation means accepting whatever settings menu the developer left you, or waiting for the developer to change the product. Good ideas stay trapped inside single products. Communities can't contribute back.

In WE, experiences are defined as declarative descriptions of layout, data, and behaviour — not as executable code. This makes them:

- **Inspectable** — you can see exactly how an experience is built
- **Remixable** — take a governance panel from one experience, a content feed from another, combine them into your own
- **Forkable** — adopt someone else's experience, modify it, publish your version back
- **Safe to share** — declarative descriptions carry no hidden executable risk

The interface becomes something a community can sculpt, not just accept.

---

### 4. A shared vocabulary, infinite compositions

AI will soon make it trivially easy to generate custom interfaces on demand. This creates a hidden trap: if every community generates its own custom data formats for posts, events, resources, and media — the result is thousands of incompatible representations of the same things. The more flexible the generation, the more fragmented the ecosystem.

WE uses a standardised set of content building blocks — text, audio, image, video, collection, and ~20 others — as a shared vocabulary. Communities compose these blocks in any combination (and AI can compose endlessly varied experiences on top of them), but the blocks themselves are universal. Any community can read any other community's content, query their libraries, and build on their structures — without any upfront coordination.

The alphabet is fixed. The sentences are free.

---

### 5. Tools that grow with you

Communities evolve. What works for 30 people breaks at 300. What serves casual discussion isn't enough for serious governance. What supports knowledge-sharing doesn't support economic coordination.

Most platforms are designed to stay the same. Changing platforms means migrating — losing history, re-onboarding every member, rebuilding workflows, severing social continuity. So communities stay stuck with tools they've outgrown, because the cost of change is too high.

Because experiences in WE are composable and data is separated from interface, communities can adopt new governance mechanisms, moderation tools, signalling systems, or economic flows without migration. A community might start with a simple voting mechanism, hit its limits, and switch to quadratic voting — without losing a single post or relationship. The environment grows as the community grows.

---

### 6. Signals that fit what your community actually cares about

Every platform gives the same signal vocabulary: likes, upvotes, stars. These don't fit what most communities actually care about. A research community needs to signal methodological quality separately from practical relevance. A governance community needs weighted votes. A creative community needs different feedback than a news community. But the signals are hardcoded — take it or leave it.

Communities in WE define their own signal types: custom names, icons, display styles (toggle, up/down, star rating, slider), and aggregation methods. A community can start with a simple like and evolve to weighted voting as it matures — without losing historical data. Because signal types carry declared meaning, signals from different communities can be normalised and compared. Cross-community content carries its context with it.

---

### 7. AI as a genuine collaborator

AI assistants today can describe changes, suggest features, write code — but they're structurally outside the product. They can't reshape an interface, modify governance logic, or adapt to a community's evolving needs. Locked, opaque apps make AI a spectator.

WE is designed to be an environment AI can actively participate in shaping. Experiences have a concrete, interpretable structure. Data uses shared block types with stable semantics. This gives AI a real surface to work on:

- Generate a new experience from a description in plain language
- Edit a layout in response to community feedback
- Propose and preview a governance change before it's adopted
- Help communities discover and adapt relevant experiences from the marketplace

AI stops being a chatbot beside the environment and becomes a collaborator inside it.

---

### 8. A marketplace that makes the ecosystem smarter

Today, good coordination patterns get trapped inside individual products. One community discovers a better way to run proposals, surface important discussions, or allocate shared resources — but that discovery rarely escapes the product it was built inside. Experimentation doesn't compound.

Distributed experimentation with compounding improvement is how social infrastructure should work. The WE marketplace distributes not just themes and widgets but governance mechanisms, signalling systems, moderation tools, knowledge interfaces, and economic flows — as reusable, forkable, evolvable components. A community that develops a better participatory budgeting flow publishes it. Another community adopts and improves it. The pattern spreads.

No central product team decides what all communities get. The ecosystem learns.

---

## What this adds up to

Most online communities today are socially alive but structurally constrained. They can grow, create, and self-organise — but they can't meaningfully evolve the software conditions they live inside.

WE is an attempt to change that. Not by building a better platform, but by giving communities a shared environment where the tools themselves become something that can be owned, remixed, and evolved over time.

The deeper bet: if the right infrastructure exists, communities will discover forms of coordination, governance, and collective intelligence that no platform team would have designed for them — and those discoveries will spread.

That is what WE is for.
