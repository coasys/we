## FOR COMMUNITIES

_Full depth page_

---

### Heading

A community environment you actually control.

### Opening

Most digital communities today are socially alive but structurally constrained.

You can shape your culture, your tone, your norms. But the deeper layer — the software conditions you actually live inside — is almost always fixed by someone else:

- The feed logic is fixed.
- The governance model is fixed.
- The moderation tools are fixed.
- The interfaces are fixed.
- The payment and resource flows are fixed.

Even when a platform offers plugins or admin controls, you're still customising within a house you don't own. The foundations belong to someone else. And when their incentives shift — and they always do — you pay the price.

WE is a different kind of starting point.

---

### What WE gives your community

**Your data, your history**
Everything your community creates lives in decentralised infrastructure you control, powered by the AD4M protocol. No central server to shut down. No platform to hold your history hostage. Uninstall an experience and your data stays. Switch to a better tool and your entire history comes with you.

Your identity and relationships aren't owned by the platform either. One persistent, portable identity moves with you across every experience inside WE — and across communities. Your reputation, your connections, your context: yours.

**Tools that evolve with you**
WE experiences are built from structured templates — not locked code. That means you can start from a template that fits your community today and adapt it as you grow. Swap in a better governance mechanism. Change how information surfaces. Adjust what signals mean in your context.

You don't need a developer to do most of this. You don't need to migrate to a new platform and start over. And you don't lose your history when you change.

**Bring your coordination into one place**
Discussion, governance, shared knowledge, resource coordination, payments — these don't have to live in six different apps. In WE, they coexist in one environment under one identity. Context doesn't get lost between tools because there's no gap to lose it in.

**Learn from other communities**
When another community figures out a better way to run proposals, surface important signals, or manage a shared treasury, that pattern can be published to the WE marketplace and adopted by anyone. Your community benefits from the discoveries of others. Your discoveries can improve the ecosystem too.

---

### What this looks like in practice

A mutual aid network starts with a simple discussion and resource-sharing template. As it grows, members want clearer proposal workflows and a shared treasury view. In WE, they adopt those modules without migrating platforms or losing their history. When they build a better participatory budgeting flow, they publish it. Three other communities adopt and improve it.

A learning community builds not just a forum but an evolving knowledge map — linked concepts, curated pathways, multiple views over the same shared knowledge base. Newcomers see guided pathways. Experts see denser technical maps. Both are lenses over the same underlying data.

A neighbourhood collective brings together discussion, local coordination, resource offers and needs, and a shared calendar — all in one environment, all connected by the same identity and data layer.

---

### The deeper point

WE starts from a simple conviction:

_Communities should be able to evolve not just what they say, but the interfaces, incentives, and institutions through which they relate._

That means less dependence on rigid platforms, less lock-in, more experimentation, more portability, and more collective ownership over the digital environments communities live inside.

## FOR BUILDERS

_Full depth page_

---

### Heading

Stop rebuilding the 80%. Start only building what's actually yours.

### Opening

Every team building social tools faces the same hidden problem.

The features that make your project genuinely novel — a unique governance mechanism, a new way to signal relevance, a different model for resource coordination — represent maybe 20% of what you need to build. The other 80% is infrastructure almost every social tool needs: identity, profiles, posts, threads, feeds, roles, notifications, moderation, content composition, data storage.

That 80% gets rebuilt from scratch, in isolation, by every team in the space. The result is a graveyard of incompatible systems, duplicated effort, and experiences that can't interoperate even when they're trying to solve adjacent problems.

WE is designed to change that.

---

### Shared infrastructure, not shared constraints

WE provides the 80% as shared, open infrastructure. Every community using WE gets identity, data continuity, a content composition system, a design system, and a component ecosystem — built in, available, and consistent.

That means when you build for WE, you're building the 20%: the coordination logic, the governance mechanism, the novel interface, the unique signalling system that makes your project actually interesting.

You publish it as a module, a widget, or a template. Other communities can adopt it, fork it, improve it, and publish improvements back. The whole ecosystem gets smarter.

This isn't just a developer convenience. It's a structural shift in how social tool innovation works — from isolated products competing through lock-in, to a compounding commons where good ideas spread.

---

### What you can build

**Templates**
A template is a complete community environment defined as a JSON schema. It specifies which modules to include, how they're laid out, how they're styled, and what interactions are possible. Templates can be forked, remixed, and versioned.

A community adopts your template, customises it to fit their context, and publishes their fork. The pattern evolves through use rather than remaining frozen inside one product.

**Modules**
Modules are composable coordination primitives — governance mechanisms, signalling systems, payment flows, knowledge interfaces, moderation tools. A module built once can be installed by any community, used across multiple experiences, and improved by anyone.

**Widgets and components**
Reusable UI building blocks contributed to the design system. Build once, available everywhere.

**Block types**
WE's block system is the shared vocabulary for content — the standard building blocks that every experience can read and render: text, images, audio, video, checklists, polls, collections, maps, and more. New block types can be contributed to the marketplace, extending what every community can compose and express.

**Experiences**
Full environments — complete UIs and coordination stacks — that communities can install and run. Think less "app" and more "lens over shared data." Two music experiences aren't fighting for lock-in; they're both views over the same library. Users can run both, mix components from each, and their data is never trapped.

---

### Why the schema approach matters

Experiences in WE are defined as JSON schemas — not compiled, sealed code. This is a deliberate architectural choice with several consequences:

**Inspectable and auditable.** Anyone can read a template and understand what it does before installing it. There are no hidden behaviours. That's a trust primitive most platforms don't have.

**AI-native.** Structured schemas are something language models can read, reason about, and generate reliably. WE isn't bolting AI onto a legacy system — it's designed from the ground up so AI can actively work inside the environment: reading structures, modifying templates, generating previews. This becomes a significant capability advantage over locked, compiled software.

**Framework agnostic.** The schema system doesn't tie you or the ecosystem to any single frontend framework. Solid is the current default, but the architecture supports others. Build on top of an open specification, not a proprietary runtime.

**Safe to share.** Declarative schemas can be shared, versioned, and forked without the risks of executing arbitrary code. The composability model is safe by design.

---

### The marketplace as an evolutionary engine

The WE marketplace isn't a place to sell themes. It's the mechanism by which the ecosystem evolves.

When you publish something — a governance module, a novel signalling system, a content block type — you're contributing to shared infrastructure that every community can build on. Communities adopt your work, discover its limitations in real use, fork it, improve it, publish improvements. You benefit from their improvements too.

This is how social tool innovation should work: distributed experimentation, rapid adoption of good patterns, compounding improvement across the whole ecosytem rather than siloed roadmaps owned by individual companies.

---

### Two-sided network effects

As more builders contribute modules and templates, the environment becomes richer for communities. As more communities use WE, there are more contexts in which your module gets real-world testing, feedback, and improvement. Each side of the ecosystem strengthens the other.

That's a healthier model for building in this space than trying to capture a community permanently inside your product.

## HOW IT WORKS

_Full depth page_

---

### Heading

A shared vocabulary for community software.

### The core insight: separate data from interface

Most social software bundles data and interface together inseparably. Your posts live inside the app. The app's logic decides how to display them, who can see them, what you can do with them. Switch apps and you start over.

WE takes a different approach: **data and interface are explicitly separated.**

Your data — posts, discussions, governance records, knowledge structures — lives in your own AD4M perspective. Experiences in WE are lenses over that data: different ways of viewing, interacting with, and organising the same underlying information. Install a new experience and it reads your existing data. Uninstall one and nothing is lost.

This is what makes WE experiences remixable, forkable, and portable. The interface isn't the data. It's just one way of seeing it.

---

### The block system: a shared content vocabulary

One of the core problems in building social tools is fragmentation at the content level. If every community generates its own schema for a music track, a post, a calendar event, or a resource offer, those 1000 incompatible schemas make cross-community sharing essentially impossible — even when communities are trying to do the same things.

WE resolves this with a shared block system: a standardised set of content primitives that every experience can read and render.

**Core block types include:**

| Block Type        | Purpose                                   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `TextBlock`       | Paragraphs, headings, lists, quotes, code |
| `ImageBlock`      | Photos, diagrams, artwork                 |
| `AudioBlock`      | Music, podcasts, voice memos              |
| `VideoBlock`      | Clips, streams, tutorials                 |
| `FileBlock`       | Documents, binary files                   |
| `CodeBlock`       | Source code, config                       |
| `TableBlock`      | Data tables, spreadsheets                 |
| `ChecklistBlock`  | Todo items, ingredient lists              |
| `MapBlock`        | Locations, routes                         |
| `CalendarBlock`   | Events, schedules                         |
| `PollBlock`       | Polls, votes, surveys                     |
| `CollectionBlock` | Playlists, albums, folders, galleries     |
| `LinkBlock`       | Bookmarks, previews                       |
| `EmbedBlock`      | External content                          |

Think of these as the alphabet. The alphabet is fixed. The sentences are free.

Any community can compose these blocks into any structure. Any experience can read and render them. New block types can be contributed to the marketplace, extending what every community can express without breaking existing content.

---

### Semantic roles: meaning without fragmentation

The same block can mean different things in different contexts. An `AudioBlock` is a music track in one community and a podcast episode in another. WE handles this through semantic roles — lightweight labels that give blocks domain-specific meaning without modifying the underlying structure.

```
AudioBlock {
  title: "Episode 42"
  src: "..."
  semanticRoles: ["podcast://episode", "community://featured"]
}
```

Multiple roles on one block. No duplication in the graph. Each community assigns the meaning that fits its context. Different experiences query by role — "find all podcast episodes" or "find all featured content" — without needing the data to be structured differently.

---

### Templates: experiences as schemas

Every experience in WE is defined as a JSON schema — a structured, human-readable description of:

- which components and modules to include
- how they're laid out
- how they're styled
- what interactions are possible
- what data to read and how to display it

This means experiences are:

**Inspectable.** You can read a template and understand exactly what it does before running it. No hidden logic.

**Forkable.** Copy a template, change one section, publish the fork. The original community's setup is unaffected. Yours immediately has a working base.

**AI-readable.** Language models can parse, reason about, and generate valid schemas. AI can help you customise your community's environment from natural language descriptions — not by writing code, but by working in the same structured format the system already speaks.

**Framework-agnostic.** The schema specification isn't tied to any single frontend framework. Solid is the current default renderer, but the architecture is open.

---

### The AD4M foundation

Everything in WE sits on AD4M — the Agent-Centric Distributed Application Meta-protocol.

AD4M provides:

- **Sovereign identity.** Every agent has a persistent, self-owned identity that works across communities and experiences. Your profile, relationships, and reputation aren't locked inside any single app.
- **Agent-centric data.** Your data lives in your own AD4M perspective — a personal graph of everything connected to your identity. No central server owns it.
- **Peer-to-peer coordination.** Communities can operate without depending on any platform's servers.
- **Semantic interoperability.** A shared predicate vocabulary lets content created by entirely different tools be understood and rendered by WE — without any prior coordination.

WE adds on top:

- A shared design and component system
- Schema-driven, remixable experiences
- A marketplace for modules, templates, and block types
- Continuity across many experiences in one environment
- AI-friendly structures for customisation and evolution

---

### Why a meta-app, not many separate apps

A natural question: if AD4M makes decentralised apps possible, why not just build lots of separate apps on AD4M?

The answer is that separate apps recover sovereignty at the infrastructure layer but recreate fragmentation at the experience layer. Each app still:

- builds its own components from scratch
- fractures identity and data across contexts
- can't easily share modules with the others
- resets innovation at its own boundary

WE provides a shared experiential layer on top of AD4M. Modules improve the whole ecosystem, not just one isolated product. Innovation compounds instead of resetting per app. Communities can evolve without repeatedly starting from zero.

That's the structural advantage of the meta-app design.

### Heading

What communities actually do with WE.

### Intro

Abstract ideas land better when you can see them in use. Here are a few ways communities are using — or could use — WE today.

---

### Example 1: A music community that can actually evolve

A small music community starts with a basic listening experience: a shared library, playlists, a player bar, and artist pages. It works well.

Six months later, a second creator publishes a new music experience with better social curation — collaborative playlists, listening history, shared recommendations. In any normal world, the community would face a choice: stick with what they have, or migrate and lose their library context.

In WE, both experiences can coexist. Community members try the new curation interface without abandoning the old library. They keep one identity, one data layer, one history. Some members use both. Over time the community gravitates toward the features that actually work for them.

The deeper point: **music interfaces become remixable lenses over shared data, not sealed products competing for lock-in.**

---

### Example 2: A governance stack that grows with the community

A local collective starts with a simple discussion and proposal template: posts, comments, a lightweight voting mechanism, a shared calendar.

As the community grows to 200 members, the simple majority vote starts causing problems. Louder voices dominate. Small decisions take as long as big ones. The community wants something more nuanced.

In WE, they don't start over. They browse the marketplace, find a quadratic voting module another community published, read how it works, and adopt it. Two weeks later, after testing, they fork it to adjust the weighting to fit their context and publish their version back.

A year later, their fork has been adopted by 14 other communities and improved three times.

**The governance stack evolved with the community. The community's history, identity, and memory stayed intact the whole time.**

---

### Example 3: A knowledge base with multiple views

A research group builds a collaborative knowledge environment. Instead of posts and comments, they create linked concept maps — structured notes connected by relationships, with signals for relevance, confidence, and novelty attached to each node.

Different members want to see this differently. Newcomers want guided pathways through the material. Senior researchers want dense, interconnected views. A subgroup focused on a specific topic wants a filtered lens over just that corner of the graph.

In WE, these are different experiences layered over the same underlying knowledge. The data doesn't change. Each view is just a different way of seeing it. Add a new experience, remove one you don't use — the knowledge graph persists regardless.

**The research group built not just a conversation space but an evolving shared memory.**

---

### Example 4: A personal workspace from community tools

WE is community-first, but the same model scales down to individual use.

A person might assemble a personal WE workspace combining: a music library from one experience, a writing and notes flow from another, a task view from a third, a sidebar connected to their community's shared space, and a knowledge map from a research group they belong to.

This is the meta-app idea made tangible. Instead of juggling six disconnected tools, they have one evolving environment made of interoperable parts — all operating under one identity, all sharing the same data layer.

---

### Example 5: A builder publishes once, improves everything

A developer frustrated by rebuilding proposal mechanics for the third time writes a clean governance module for WE: structured proposals, a configurable voting mechanism, a decision log.

They publish it to the marketplace. Within a month, 30 communities are using it. Feedback comes in: one community found an edge case in the tie-breaking logic, another extended it with a quorum threshold, a third built a visualisation widget for the decision log.

The original developer merges the improvements they like. The communities keep their forks. The module is now substantially better than anything one person could have built in isolation.

**That's the 80% problem solved — not by one team building everything, but by a community of builders sharing the foundations.**

---

### The pattern underneath

Every example here shows the same deeper structure:

- Data is separate from interface, so the interface can evolve without breaking anything
- Shared block types mean content is readable by any experience without coordination
- Templates are forkable, so good patterns spread without imposing uniformity
- The marketplace makes discovery, adoption, and improvement possible across the whole ecosystem
- Identity and data continuity mean communities never have to start over just to grow

That is the practical value of WE.
