## FOR COMMUNITIES

_Full depth page_

---

### Heading

Tools that belong to your community, not a platform.

### Opening

Most digital communities today are socially alive but structurally constrained.

You can shape your culture, your tone, your norms. But the deeper layer — the software conditions you actually live inside — is almost always fixed by someone else:

- The feed logic is fixed.
- The governance model is fixed.
- The moderation tools are fixed.
- The interface is fixed.
- What counts as a signal is fixed.

Even when a platform offers plugins or admin controls, you're still customising within a house you don't own. The foundations belong to someone else. And when their incentives shift — and they always do — you pay the price.

WE is built differently.

---

### What WE makes possible

**Your data, your history**
Everything your community creates lives in decentralised infrastructure you control, powered by the AD4M protocol. No central server to shut down. No platform to hold your history hostage. Uninstall an experience and your data stays. Switch to a different interface and your entire history comes with you.

Your identity and relationships aren't owned by the platform either. One persistent, portable identity moves with you across every experience inside WE — and across communities. Your reputation, your connections, your context: yours.

**Tools that grow with you**
WE experiences are built from structured templates — not locked code. Start from a template that fits your community today, adapt it as your needs change. Adjust how information surfaces. Define what kinds of signals matter in your context. Fork an experience that's mostly right and change the parts that aren't.

You don't need to migrate to a new platform to do this. You don't lose your history when you change.

**Your coordination in one place**
Discussion, shared knowledge, resource coordination, community signals — these don't have to live in separate apps. In WE, they coexist in one environment under one identity. Context doesn't get lost between tools because there's no gap to lose it in.

**Design signals that mean something to your community**
Not all communities surface information the same way. WE's signalling system lets communities define their own signal types — what counts as important, relevant, urgent, or well-crafted — rather than accepting the platform's one-size-fits-all defaults. Your community's values shape how attention flows.

**Learn from others, contribute back**
When another community builds something better, that pattern can be published and adopted. WE's growing marketplace is designed to let useful coordination patterns spread — not stay locked inside the group that discovered them. Your community's discoveries can improve the ecosystem too.

---

### What this looks like in practice

A local collective starts with a simple discussion and resource-sharing template. As the community grows, members want clearer ways to surface urgent needs and signal collective support. They use WE's signalling system to define exactly what those signals mean for their context — not repurposing a generic "like" button but creating something that carries real meaning for their culture.

A learning community builds not just a forum but an evolving knowledge environment — structured notes, linked concepts, multiple views over the same shared knowledge base. Newcomers see guided pathways. Experts see denser, more interconnected maps. Both are lenses over the same underlying data.

A neighbourhood group brings together discussion, local coordination, resource offers and needs, and a shared calendar — all in one environment, all connected by the same identity and memory.

---

### The deeper point

WE starts from a simple conviction:

_Communities should be able to evolve not just what they say, but the interfaces, incentives, and institutions through which they relate._

That means more agency over digital tools, more continuity of history and identity, and more room for communities to discover forms of organisation that actually fit who they are.

## FOR BUILDERS

_Full depth page_

---

### Heading

Stop rebuilding the 80%. Start only building what's actually yours.

### Opening

Every team building social tools faces the same hidden problem.

The features that make your project genuinely novel — a unique way to surface signals, a different model for resource coordination, a new kind of community interface — represent maybe 20% of what you need to build. The other 80% is infrastructure almost every social tool needs: identity, profiles, posts, threads, feeds, roles, notifications, moderation, content composition, data storage.

That 80% gets rebuilt from scratch, in isolation, by every team in the space. The result is a graveyard of incompatible systems, duplicated effort, and experiences that can't interoperate even when they're solving adjacent problems.

WE is designed to change that.

---

### Shared infrastructure, not shared constraints

WE provides the 80% as shared, open infrastructure. Every community using WE gets identity, data continuity, a content composition system, a design system, and a component ecosystem — built in, available, and consistent.

That means when you build for WE, you're building the 20%: the novel interface, the unique signalling logic, the coordination pattern that makes your project genuinely interesting.

You publish it as a template, a widget, or a block type. Others can adopt it, fork it, improve it, and publish improvements back. The whole ecosystem gets smarter from work you've already done.

This isn't just a developer convenience. It's a structural shift in how social tool innovation works — from isolated products competing through lock-in, to a compounding commons where good ideas spread.

---

### What you can build

**Templates**
A template is a complete community environment defined as a JSON schema — which components to include, how they're laid out, how they're styled, what interactions are possible. Templates can be forked, remixed, and AI-generated.

A community adopts your template, customises it to fit their context, and publishes their fork. The pattern evolves through real use rather than remaining frozen inside one product.

**Widgets and components**
Reusable UI building blocks contributed to the shared ecosystem. Build once — available to every community and experience that wants them.

**Block types**
WE's block system is the shared vocabulary for content: the standard building blocks every experience can read and render. New block types can be contributed to extend what every community can compose and express — not just in your experience, but across the whole ecosystem.

**Signalling systems**
Communities can define custom signal types — what counts as relevant, urgent, high quality, or worth amplifying in their context. Build reusable signalling patterns other communities can adopt and adapt.

**Experiences**
Full environments — complete UIs and coordination stacks — that communities can install and run. Think less "app" and more "lens over shared data." Two music experiences aren't fighting for lock-in; they're both views over the same library. Users can run both, mix components from each, and their data is never trapped.

---

### Why the schema approach matters

Experiences in WE are defined as JSON schemas — not compiled, sealed code. This is a deliberate architectural choice with several significant consequences:

**Inspectable and auditable.** Anyone can read a template and understand what it does before installing it. No hidden behaviours. That's a trust primitive most platforms don't have.

**AI-native by design.** Structured schemas are something language models can read, reason about, and generate reliably. WE isn't bolting AI onto a legacy system — it's designed from the ground up so AI can actively work inside the environment: reading structures, understanding available data, generating and previewing templates. AI already works today to generate templates from natural language descriptions of what a community needs, including reasoning about data structures from perspectives created entirely outside WE.

**Framework agnostic.** The schema system doesn't tie you or the ecosystem to any single frontend framework. Solid is the current default renderer, but the architecture is open. Build on a specification, not a proprietary runtime.

**Safe to share.** Declarative schemas can be shared, versioned, and forked without the risks of executing arbitrary code. The composability model is safe by design.

---

### The marketplace as an evolutionary engine

The WE marketplace — in development — is designed as the mechanism by which the ecosystem evolves, not just a place to download themes.

When you publish something — a novel signalling pattern, a new block type, a carefully designed template — you're contributing to shared infrastructure every community can build on. Communities adopt your work, discover its limitations in real use, fork it, improve it. You benefit from their improvements too.

This is how social tool innovation should work: distributed experimentation, rapid adoption of good patterns, compounding improvement across the whole ecosystem rather than siloed roadmaps owned by individual companies.

---

### The interoperability advantage

WE sits on AD4M, which means WE experiences can read and render data from perspectives created by entirely independent tools — not just other WE experiences. A community might be using Flux for their social layer; a WE experience can understand and surface that data without any coordination between the teams that built each tool.

That's a fundamentally different model from platform interoperability agreements. Shared semantics at the protocol level means communities aren't trapped by whatever integrations a platform has chosen to build.

---

### Two-sided network effects

As more builders contribute templates and components, the environment becomes richer for communities. As more communities use WE, there are more real-world contexts in which your work gets tested, improved, and forked. Each side strengthens the other.

That's a healthier model for building in this space than trying to permanently capture a community inside your product.

## HOW IT WORKS

_Full depth page_

---

### Heading

A shared vocabulary for community software.

### The core insight: separate data from interface

Most social software bundles data and interface together inseparably. Your posts live inside the app. The app's logic decides how to display them, who can see them, what you can do with them. Switch apps and you start over.

WE takes a different approach: **data and interface are explicitly separated.**

Your data — posts, discussions, knowledge structures, shared resources — lives in your own AD4M perspective. Experiences in WE are lenses over that data: different ways of viewing, interacting with, and organising the same underlying information. Install a new experience and it reads your existing data. Uninstall one and nothing is lost.

This is what makes WE experiences remixable, forkable, and portable. The interface isn't the data. It's just one way of seeing it.

---

### The block system: a shared content vocabulary

One of the core problems in building social tools is fragmentation at the content level. If every community generates its own schema for a music track, a post, a calendar event, or a resource offer, you end up with thousands of incompatible structures representing the same things. Cross-community sharing becomes essentially impossible — not because the data doesn't exist, but because no one agreed on what to call it.

WE resolves this with a shared block system: a standardised set of content primitives that every experience can read and render.

**Core block types:**

| Block Type        | Purpose                                   |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| `TextBlock`       | Paragraphs, headings, lists, quotes, code |
| `ImageBlock`      | Photos, diagrams, artwork                 |
| `AudioBlock`      | Music, podcasts, voice memos              |
| `VideoBlock`      | Clips, streams, tutorials                 |
| `FileBlock`       | Documents, binary files                   |
| `CodeBlock`       | Source code, config                       |
| `TableBlock`      | Data tables, spreadsheets                 |
| `ChecklistBlock`  | Todo items, ingredient lists              |
| `MapBlock`        | Locations, routes                         |
| `CalendarBlock`   | Events, schedules                         |
| `PollBlock`       | Polls, votes, surveys                     |
| `CollectionBlock` | Playlists, albums, folders, galleries     |
| `LinkBlock`       | Bookmarks, previews                       |
| `EmbedBlock`      | External content                          |

Think of these as the alphabet. The alphabet is fixed. The sentences are free.

Any community can compose these blocks into any structure. Any experience can read and render them. New block types can be contributed to extend what every community can express — without breaking or invalidating anything that already exists.

---

### Templates: experiences as schemas

Every experience in WE is defined as a JSON schema — a structured, human-readable description of which components to include, how they're laid out, how they're styled, and what interactions are possible.

This means experiences are:

**Inspectable.** You can read a template and understand exactly what it does before running it. No hidden logic, no black-box behaviours.

**Forkable.** Copy a template, change what you need, publish the fork. The original is unaffected. Yours immediately has a working base.

**AI-readable.** Language models can parse, reason about, and generate valid schemas. AI can help communities customise their environment from natural language — not by writing code, but by working in the same structured format the system already speaks. This works today, including for communities whose data comes from AD4M perspectives created by entirely different tools.

**Framework-agnostic.** The schema specification isn't tied to any single frontend framework. Solid is the current default renderer, but the architecture is open to others.

---

### The signalling system

Communities don't all surface information the same way, and a generic "like" or "upvote" flattens nuance that matters.

WE's signalling system lets communities define their own signal types — what counts as relevant, urgent, well-crafted, or worth amplifying in their specific context. Signals are first-class objects in the system: they can be attached to any content, queried by experiences, and used to shape how information surfaces.

A research community might signal for _epistemic confidence_ and _novelty_. A mutual aid network might signal for _urgency_ and _capacity to help_. A creative collective might signal for _craft_ and _resonance_. Each community defines what attention means for them.

---

### The AD4M foundation

Everything in WE sits on AD4M — the Agent-Centric Distributed Application Meta-protocol.

AD4M provides:

- **Sovereign identity.** Every agent has a persistent, self-owned identity that works across communities and experiences. Your profile, relationships, and reputation aren't locked inside any single app.
- **Agent-centric data.** Your data lives in your own AD4M perspective — a personal graph of everything connected to your identity. No central server owns it.
- **Peer-to-peer coordination.** Communities can operate without depending on any platform's servers.
- **Semantic interoperability.** A shared predicate vocabulary means content created by entirely different tools can be understood and rendered by WE without prior coordination between builders.

WE adds on top:

- A shared design and component system
- Schema-driven, remixable experiences
- A block system as a shared content vocabulary
- A signalling system communities can customise
- AI-driven template generation
- Sub-app integration — existing AD4M apps run inside WE, sharing identity and data
- A growing marketplace for templates, widgets, and block types

---

### Why a meta-app, not many separate apps

A natural question: if AD4M makes decentralised apps possible, why not just build lots of separate apps on AD4M?

The answer is that separate apps recover sovereignty at the infrastructure layer but recreate fragmentation at the experience layer. Each app still:

- rebuilds its own components and workflows from scratch
- fractures identity and data across separate contexts
- can't share modules or patterns with other apps
- resets innovation at its own boundary

WE provides a shared experiential layer on top of AD4M. Components and templates improve the whole ecosystem, not just one isolated product. Innovation compounds instead of resetting per app. Communities evolve without repeatedly starting from zero.

That's the structural advantage of the meta-app design.

### Example 1: A music community that can actually evolve

A small music community starts with a basic listening experience: a shared library, playlists, a player bar, and artist pages. It works well.

Six months later, a second creator publishes a new music experience with better social curation — collaborative playlists, listening history, shared recommendations. In any other world, the community faces a choice: stay with what they have, or migrate and lose their history.

In WE, both experiences can coexist. Members try the new curation interface without abandoning the old library. They keep one identity, one data layer, one history. Some use both. Over time the community gravitates toward the features that actually work for them — not the ones they're locked into.

**Music interfaces become remixable lenses over shared data, not sealed products competing for lock-in.**

---

### Example 2: A community that designs its own signals

A collective is frustrated. Their discussion space has hundreds of posts but no reliable way to surface what actually matters. Upvotes feel blunt. Everything marked "important" starts to mean nothing.

They open WE's signal designer and create three custom signal types: _needs response_ (for posts that genuinely require collective attention), _worth archiving_ (for insights the community wants to keep), and _tentative_ (for ideas that are speculative and shouldn't be treated as settled). They configure the experience to visually weight these signals in the feed.

Within two weeks, the texture of their discussions changes. The signals carry real meaning because the community defined them.

They publish their signal configuration as a template. A related collective adopts it, adjusts one signal type, and publishes the fork.

**Attention flows the way the community decides — not the way the platform assumes.**

---

### Example 3: A knowledge base with multiple views

A research group builds a collaborative knowledge environment. Instead of posts and comments, they create linked concept maps — structured notes connected by relationships, with signals for relevance, confidence, and novelty attached to each node.

Different members want to see this differently. Newcomers want guided pathways. Senior researchers want dense, interconnected views. A subgroup focused on one topic wants a filtered lens over just that corner of the graph.

In WE, these are different experiences layered over the same underlying knowledge. The data doesn't change. Each view is just a different way of seeing it. Add a new experience, remove one you don't use — the knowledge graph persists regardless.

**The research group built not just a conversation space but an evolving shared memory.**

---

### Example 4: Flux inside WE

A community has been running their social activity in Flux — discussions, posts, shared perspectives — and wants to bring that into a richer WE environment without starting over.

Because both Flux and WE are built on AD4M, WE can read and render Flux perspectives directly. The community's AI-generated WE template understands the data structures Flux created, surfaces them through WE's design system and block renderer, and integrates them alongside WE-native content in one coherent environment.

No migration. No data loss. The community's history in Flux becomes part of their WE environment.

**Interoperability at the protocol level means communities aren't trapped by the tool they started with.**

---

### Example 5: A personal workspace from community tools

WE is community-first, but the same model scales down to individual use.

A person assembles a personal WE workspace: a music library from one experience, a writing and notes flow from another, a task view from a third, a sidebar connected to their community's shared space, and a knowledge map from a research group they belong to.

Instead of juggling six disconnected tools, they have one evolving environment made of interoperable parts — all under one identity, all sharing the same data layer.

**The meta-app idea made tangible: your tools compose rather than compete.**

---

### Example 6: A builder improves the whole ecosystem

A developer building community tools has rebuilt a basic content composition flow three times across three different projects. Each time, mostly the same code. Each time, siloed in a product no one else can build on.

This time, they build it once as a WE widget — a rich block composer with a clean API and sensible defaults. They publish it. Within a few months, other builders are using it as a foundation. One improves the mobile interaction. Another adds a new block type for structured data entry. A third builds a visualisation layer on top.

The original developer uses these improvements. So does everyone else who adopted the widget.

**That's the 80% problem in reverse — shared infrastructure that gets better the more people use it.**

---

### The pattern underneath

Every example here shows the same deeper structure:

- Data is separate from interface, so the interface can evolve without breaking anything
- A shared block vocabulary means content is readable by any experience without coordination
- Templates are forkable, so good patterns spread without imposing uniformity
- Signalling is community-defined, so attention flows to what actually matters locally
- AD4M interoperability means the ecosystem is open — tools built independently can still work together
- Identity and data continuity mean communities never have to start over just to grow

That is the practical value of WE.
